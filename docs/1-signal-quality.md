# Signal quality, 2012–2026

Tested against Bitcoin’s next 12 months. A buy call is counted right when that year gained at least 50%. A sell call is counted right when that year was flat or down. A full weekly instruction is scored on the five rungs, with exact credit for the right rung and half credit for one rung off.

The hurdle matters. From 2012 through September 2025, 60% of weeks were already followed by a 50% gain. The best constant instruction, “always buy big,” scores about 54% on the rung scale. A signal has to beat that.

**Signal Quality uses the low end of the estimate, not the raw win rate.** Four wins in four tries is a perfect list and still a Medium grade, because four tries cannot hold an 85% claim. On that standard, Very High and High are empty.

Those published floors match a Wilson 90% lower bound. An external check on 26 September 2026 reproduced them: 59.7% for 4 of 4, 46.0% for 6 of 8, and 48.2% for 11 of 16. The same bound is 74.7% for 8 of 8, 78.7% for 10 of 10, 80.3% for 11 of 11, and 80.4% for 19 of 20. A gate of eight straight wins cannot be an 80% floor. Sixteen straight wins are what clear the study's own 85% Very High line (floor 85.5%). The buy cross has fired about every 2.8 years. Eleven completed wins, with none missed, would land in the mid-2040s at the earliest. One miss moves the 80% line to 19 of 20.

Readings were weekly Friday closes. Friday is that weekly sample: one reading per week, using a gold close from the same market week. The study did not compare weekdays, and it has no result that Friday is a better day to act. The power-law trend is refit each week on data up to that week. On-chain series were checked against the desk tape for 24 September 2026: MVRV Z-score 1.03, NUPL 0.37, reserve risk 0.000648, VDD 0.738, Fear & Greed 71 Greed, Puell about 1.03. The rebuilt macro-pressure gauge prints about +4 on 25 September 2026, against about +7 on the desk.

## Kept together

Use the power law and the 52-week gold z-score as one event, the buy cross:

- Arm when the z-score is at or below −90 and price is at least 20% under the expanding trend.
- Fire when the z-score later crosses back above zero.

Completed fires: 24 Jul 2015, 3 May 2019, 31 Jul 2020, 17 Mar 2023. The open fire is 18 Sep 2026, near $81,000. The desk’s list has July 2015, April 2019, March 2023, and September 2026. May 2019 is the same episode as April. July 2020 is an extra fire the list left out, and it was the strongest of the four completed years (+269%).

Requiring a prior break under the realized price would have dropped July 2020. That gate has not filtered out a loser. It is not part of the quality rule.

The sell rule that matches the written thresholds (55% above the trend and z-score at least +40, then a 10% drop from that spell’s peak) fires in Jun 2013, Apr 2014, Aug 2014, Sep 2017, Mar 2018, May 2018, May 2021, and Dec 2021. It does not print Apr 2013, Dec 2017, Jul 2019, or Mar 2021. Those months are peaks. The live rule turns on before the peak and turns off after a large part of the drop. Do not treat the four famous months as the backtest.

The 6-of-8 line counts fires that were followed by a flat or down year. It does not count declines the rule missed. The external check below adds that recall. A sell rule needs both numbers on the same line. The record is about the next 12 months. A pause of a few Fridays does not use that record.

## Not scored

Spot demand has no series on the desk, and none was added here.

ETF flow starts in January 2024. That is not four completed 12-month episodes. Show it as context, and as a regime flag on every calibration from before 2024. Corporate treasury buying belongs in the same note. Neither is a timer.

Strategy purchases start in 2020. No purchase tape was scored, so there is no accuracy number.

Macro liquidity (Fed net liquidity, global M2, real yields, the dollar, high-yield credit spreads) was not in this study. Long histories exist. If it is tested later, test it as a filter on the sell-roll pause, and watch for lag-fitting. It is not a buy timer on the evidence in hand.

## How later tests have to score

Three additions belong beside the hit rate. They are not a reason to replace the table above.

1. Payoff against the default. Score each action in coins accumulated, and in after-tax dollars once the account is known, at 1, 2, and 4 years. Keep the hit rate beside those numbers.
2. Recall on every sell rule. Count declines caught, declines missed, and how far into the decline the rule fired.
3. Independent episodes beside weekly counts. The 91 cheap weeks are four spells. A floor computed on 91 would overstate the evidence. Weekly windows overlap.

The +50% year is the timing score. It is not the goal. The first week under the realized price on 31 October 2014 was down 3% a year later and up 105% two years later. June 2022 was up 29% after one year and up 222% after two. Both fail the +50% rule. Both more than doubled inside two years. Those two-year figures are from the external check, not from the original table.

A new tranche on every separate dip also overstates how many cheap spells a bear contains. The external check found three spells in the 2022 bear across seven months, lasting 5, 11, and 9 weeks. Since 2015, a 26-week tranche would have finished in only one of six spells. Those three 2022 spells would have used 19%, 42%, and 35% of a 26-week tranche. The strategy freezes a spell-end rule before any replay of that fact. The rule is not itself a result of this check.

### The reserve against buying as cash arrives

The buy cross was 4 for 4 against the next 12 months. A reserve that sat in cash from one fire to the next lost to spending the same dollars as they arrived, in every cycle. Each fire was priced above the last, so cash carried between episodes bought fewer coins.

| Mar 2023 to Sep 2026 | 166 | $55,509 | $81,000 | 31% fewer |
| Jul 2020 to Mar 2023 | 136 | $25,574 | $27,451 | 7% fewer |
| May 2019 to Jul 2020 | 64 | $8,606 | $11,338 | 24% fewer |
| Jul 2015 to May 2019 | 196 | $921 | $5,658 | 84% fewer |

The latest row uses weekly prices through 22 May 2026. The missing summer weeks traded near $60,000 to $84,000 and do not change the sign. A reserve refilled right after one fire and held to the next did worse still: 2.0 to 19.6 times fewer coins.

The buy cross is a good moment to buy. It is a bad reason to hold cash through the years between episodes. Whether cash should wait from the arm to the fire is a different question. This check did not answer it.

Spreading the fire over three Fridays cost 1% to 13% more than buying on the fire Friday, in three of the four episodes. Spreading it over 26 weeks cost 2% to 60% more. The immediate buy matches the fire that was scored.

A regime is a run of Fridays past the −20% line or the +55% line. Runs less than 13 weeks apart are one regime. A regime wins if its median week favored the rule, in coins over the next 52 weeks.

### Gold share at the buy-cross arm

Measured on the same Friday series as the buy cross. Prices run from 23 July 2010. The z-score needs 52 weeks, and the rule first armed on 3 October 2014. There are five arms.

Gold's share uses the path from the last prior Friday, inside those 52 weeks, on which the ratio was still at or above its 52-week mean. The share is the portion of the log cheapening that came from gold rising. On the four completed arms that share is 0%. Gold was flat or down. On 21 November 2025, the open arm, the ratio had left its mean on 12 September 2025. Bitcoin then fell about 27% and gold rose about 11%. Gold's share was 24.5%. The fire was 18 September 2026.

The 15% cut sits between 0% and 24.5%. It flags the open arm and none of the completed arms. It is not a fitted return threshold. With one arm on the gold side, no cut can be graded as a hit rate. A cut of 50% would leave this arm unflagged.

### Share of a cycle's percentage gain

A cycle runs from the lowest daily close after the previous cycle high to the next high that was later followed by a drop of at least 70%, and the rise has to be at least 4x. The latest advance is included even though the drop after 6 October 2025 is about 53%, not 70%. Cycles before 2015 had no buy-cross fire.

The share is the signal's percentage gain up to the cycle high, divided by the cycle's own percentage gain from its low. The entry for the buy cross is the fire Friday. The entry for Build is the average Friday close under the realized price during that cycle. The entry for Lump in is the average Friday close at least 20% under the trend during that cycle. Holding to the high is the yardstick. The dashboard does not sell there. The sell roll does not sell coins, so it has no share of the rise.

### Sell-roll recall

The +55% band has not been reached since 2021. Cycle peaks over the trend ran +782%, +557%, +217%, and +27%. The band may stay out of reach.

From the October 2025 daily-close high of $124,824, price fell 49% by February 2026. The rule was silent. It was also silent from June 2019 to March 2020 (−61%). It fired during five of the seven declines of 40% or more since 2012.

Five of the eight fires came after price was already 44% to 60% below its high. September 2017, May 2021, and December 2021 were the fires near a top.

### Diagnostics

Power-law fit sensitivity. The September 2026 gap runs from −29% to −46% as the fit's start year moves from 2010 to 2017, with slopes from 4.95 to 5.98. The arm, 20% under the trend, holds under every one of those fits. A long-range price target taken from one fit does not.

The gold leg of the z-score. Over the year into this episode, Bitcoin fell 23% while gold rose 17%, so about 38% of the ratio's fall came from gold. Gold rose from $3,723 to $4,360. The fire week itself was led by Bitcoin: Bitcoin rose 10.4% in the week to 25 September 2026, and gold was $4,331 on 1 September and $4,360 on 21 September. Show both legs. Flag an arm or a fire that gold drove. A split of every past arm and fire is still an open test. This pass did not rebuild the z-score.

### Rules that cannot be backtested

Exit, a sale of all coins, needs a reason the cycle record does not supply. Two sell-roll fires were followed by +565% and +85%. These monitors are risk rules. They have no hit rate:

- A sustained break below a power-law floor band. That band is not the −20% line, and no floor band has been frozen.
- A lasting collapse in hash rate.
- A credible break of Bitcoin's signature scheme with no migration path.
- Prohibition across major markets.

Label them as risk rules. Do not print a confidence floor on them.
